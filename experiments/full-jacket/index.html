Direct source observation — printed page 127

Visible entry heading: “SMITH, BENJAMIN FRANKLIN (Chickasaw).”

  • The entry describes Benjamin Franklin Smith as Chickasaw agent, appointed September 17, 1823, and recounts disputes with missionaries, deputy agent Randle Malbone, Chickasaw leaders, and the Colbert family.
  • It says Smith moved the agency house in 1825 from Houlka Creek in present Chickasaw County, Mississippi, to a site about ninety miles northeast near present Cherokee, Alabama.

Load-bearing language: “Colonel Thomas L. McKenney, Superintendent of Indian Affairs and a personal friend of chief Levi Colbert, conducted an extensive investigation into Smith’s deportment in October, 1827, but made no decision regarding Smith’s conduct.”

Load-bearing language: “He then appointed John Bell of Cotton Gin Port, Mississippi, to take depositions regarding the charges.”

Load-bearing language: “Bell’s report of July 3, 1828, based on testimony of the Colbert clique, was detrimental to Smith.”

The entry says Smith remained in conflict with the Colberts and missionaries and left the Chickasaws in late 1829; it quotes Levi Colbert reacting to Smith’s departure.

Footnote / source trail visible on printed page 127

  • Footnote 1: Barr to Secretary of War, June 1824, OIA, microcopy M-234.
  • Footnote 2: Malbone to Secretary of War; McKenney to Secretary of War, May 5, 1824; “Ibid.”

Findings

  • The source explicitly places a John Bell “of Cotton Gin Port, Mississippi” in a documentary role in the Chickasaw Agency controversy: Thomas L. McKenney appointed him to take depositions, and a July 3, 1828 Bell report is cited.
  • Thomas L. McKenney appears here as Superintendent of Indian Affairs and investigator. This is a McKenney occurrence, not evidence about the separately studied McKinney surname occurrences.
  • The source creates a concrete primary-source door: Bell to Barbour, July 3, 1828, in OIA microcopy M-234.

Archival re-find — external finding aids, kept separate from source observation

  • The National Archives identifies M234 as “Letters Received by the Office of Indian Affairs, 1824–1880/1881” within Record Group 75 and states that the M234 correspondence series is digitized in the National Archives Catalog.
  • An Oklahoma Historical Society National Archives microfilm guide places Chickasaw Agency letters for 1824–1829 on NARA M234 roll 135. Because the target letter is dated July 3, 1828, roll 135 is the present best bounded retrieval target.
  • This roll placement is a finding-aid conclusion, not direct inspection of the Bell letter. Exact file number, image/frame number, handwriting, enclosures, deposition names, and physical sequence remain unresolved until the primary object is opened.

Inference — deliberately separate

  • The printed account suggests that John Bell’s July 3, 1828 report may contain or summarize depositions bearing on the Smith–Colbert conflict and therefore may name additional Chickasaw participants, witnesses, places, or agency relationships. The report itself must be opened before any such content is claimed.
  • The co-occurrence of the surnames Bell, Colbert, and McKenney in this source reflects an administrative episode. It does not by itself establish genealogy or identity with people in the Bell–McKinney Choctaw treaty-land corpus.
