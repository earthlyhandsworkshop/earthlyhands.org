Dawson reportby lantern light

The light catches a report.

On 29 January 1831, J. L. Dawson wrote from Cantonment Gibson. His report carries travel with the Choctaw and Chickasaw western exploring delegations.

The original manuscript has not yet been recovered. We are following the report through its newspaper and later printed representations.

Dawson reportnear the mouth of Blue Water

The report shifts from encounter to prospect.

[

You are carried to a relative position near the mouth of Blue Water. No exact point is supplied.

Dawson says the party was within a short distance of the mouth of Blue Water.

He says he wanted to see the mouths of Blue Water and the Washita to report on their local advantages for a possible garrison.

Intent to inspect is not proof that either mouth was reached, surveyed, selected, or built upon.

Dawson reporteast of Blue Water

Dawson turns from place to forecast.

[

The ground is now regional rather than point-like. The account is making a judgment, not locating a settlement.

Dawson says there is ample room for the whole nation in the portion east of Blue Water.

He predicts that this portion will fill with settlements first, citing winter range and comparative exposure.

This is Dawson's forecast. It is not proof that settlement had already occurred or a mapped boundary east of Blue Water.

]
