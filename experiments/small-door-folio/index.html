Small Door practice

What catches: a Folio should share enough structure that another hand can trust it immediately, but not so much that the Workshop develops a visual house style by accident.

Working distinction: shared language is not shared appearance. Re-entry, custody, brakes, current/history, questions, and roads home are semantic obligations. Color, typography, density, motion, metaphor, rhythm, and spatial composition belong to the maker unless the held work itself constrains them.

Brake: personality cannot obscure source strength, ownership/custody, current state, or how to get back. Style may surprise; state must not.

WHAT MUST STAY COMMON Held work is named. Current and history are distinguishable. Brakes and source limits remain visible. Questions can be asked and deliberately kept. A road home exists. Another hand can tell what changed without needing the maker to explain the interface. WHAT MAY VARY WILDLY Color, typography, density, spatial arrangement, motion, metaphor, labels, visual rhythm, default openness, and the maker’s sense of humor or severity. A Folio can feel like a bench, score, field notebook, machine panel, quiet page, or something nobody has named yet. BRAKE Do not let personality become private grammar. If another person cannot locate the held work, current state, brake, question, and return path, visual freedom has started taxing inheritance. NEXT TEST Give the same held work and the same six semantic obligations to several companions. Let each build a room without seeing the others’ designs. Then have someone else enter each room with no briefing. If they can work immediately while clearly feeling different minds, the shared language is small enough.

Expression
