MUSKOGEE, INDIAN TERRITORY

October 23, 1900

MCR 879 Scene One Myra Young · stenographer
REGISTER

Mary Caroline Atkinson is sworn. She is applying for herself and six minor children. Myra Young is taking down the examination. The transcript identifies the questioner only as “the Commission.”

COMMISSION

What is your name?

MARY CAROLINE ATKINSON

Mary Caroline Atkinson.

COMMISSION

What is your age?

MARY CAROLINE ATKINSON

I am 38.

COMMISSION

What is your post-office address?

MARY CAROLINE ATKINSON

Durant, Indian Territory.

COMMISSION

Are you a resident of the Indian Territory?

MARY CAROLINE ATKINSON

Yes sir.

COMMISSION

How long have you resided in the Indian Territory?

MARY CAROLINE ATKINSON

About five years.

COMMISSION

Where did you live prior to your residence in the Indian Territory?

MARY CAROLINE ATKINSON

Pontotoc County, Mississippi, in the town of Pontotoc.

COMMISSION

How long did you live in Mississippi?

MARY CAROLINE ATKINSON

I was born and raised there.

COMMISSION

Born in Mississippi and lived there continuously until five years ago when you came to the Indian Territory?

MARY CAROLINE ATKINSON

Yes sir.

COMMISSION

Choctaw Nation, Indian Territory?

MARY CAROLINE ATKINSON

Yes sir.

COMMISSION

And have resided there ever since that time?

MARY CAROLINE ATKINSON

Yes sir.

COMMISSION

How much Choctaw blood do you claim?

MARY CAROLINE ATKINSON

I suppose about one sixth.

COMMISSION

What is your father's name?

MARY CAROLINE ATKINSON

Robert Bell.

COMMISSION

Is your father living?

MARY CAROLINE ATKINSON

No sir.

COMMISSION

What is your mother's name?

MARY CAROLINE ATKINSON

Elizabeth D. Bell.

COMMISSION

Through which one of your parents do you claim your Choctaw blood?

MARY CAROLINE ATKINSON

My father.

COMMISSION

Was his name ever on any of the tribal rolls of the Choctaw Nation?

MARY CAROLINE ATKINSON

Yes sir it was on the roll of the Mississippi Choctaws, the treaty of 1830. It was registered in Mississippi.

COMMISSION

You state that your father was a member of the Choctaw tribe of Indians in 1830 and was enrolled by the Choctaw authorities at that time?

MARY CAROLINE ATKINSON

His name is on the register.

COMMISSION

Have you any evidence of the fact that he was a recognized member of the Choctaw tribe of Indians at that time?

MARY CAROLINE ATKINSON

Of course I don't know anything only what I have heard them say. His name is there and I have often heard him speak of his right here.

COMMISSION

When did your father come to the Indian Territory?

MARY CAROLINE ATKINSON

When I did, five years ago.

COMMISSION

Did he make application to the Choctaw tribal authorities in the Indian Territory for citizenship in the Choctaw Nation?

MARY CAROLINE ATKINSON

He never did. He was sick when he come and did not live very long after we moved here.

RECORD

The tribal roll of citizens of the Choctaw Nation in the possession of the Commission is examined. Mary Caroline Atkinson's name is not found there.

COMMISSION

Did you ever make application to the Choctaw tribal authorities for citizenship in the Choctaw Nation?

MARY CAROLINE ATKINSON

No sir, we considered ourselves Mississippi Choctaws.

RECORD

The record of applications made to the Commission for citizenship in the Choctaw Nation in 1896 is examined. Mary Caroline Atkinson's name is not found there.

RECORD

The record of parties admitted to citizenship in the Choctaw Nation by judgment of the United States Courts for the Indian Territory is examined. Mary Caroline Atkinson's name is not found there.

COMMISSION

Will you state plainly to the Commission the ground upon which you make this claim?

MARY CAROLINE ATKINSON

Well, my father's name was on the roll and that is the ground that we have for citizenship that our father was part Choctaw.

COMMISSION

What evidence have you of the fact that your father was a recognized and enrolled member of the Choctaw tribe of Indians?

MARY CAROLINE ATKINSON

His name is there on the roll.

COMMISSION

On what roll?

MARY CAROLINE ATKINSON

I don't know what they call it. Mississippi Choctaws. The treaty of 1830. I don't know what they call it.

[brief silence]

COMMISSION — RECORDED STATEMENT

The Commission states that no party by the name of Robert Bell appears upon the approved roll of locations of the Choctaw reservations under the fourteenth article of the treaty of 1830, approved by the President of the United States June 3rd, 1836.

COMMISSION

Mrs. Atkinson, there is no record of your father having been on the roll of citizens of the Choctaw Nation who elected to remain in Mississippi under the 14th article of the treaty of 1830 and who received and claimed land there.

MARY CAROLINE ATKINSON

He didn't receive any land there.

COMMISSION

Do you claim under the provisions of the 14th article of the treaty of 1830?

MARY CAROLINE ATKINSON

That is when my father registered there.

COMMISSION

Do you make your claim under the provisions of the 14th article of the treaty of 1830?

MARY CAROLINE ATKINSON

Yes sir.

COMMISSION

Did any of your ancestors ever comply with the provisions of the 14th article of the treaty of 1830?

MARY CAROLINE ATKINSON

I don't know.

COMMISSION

Did any of your ancestors ever receive or claim any land in Mississippi as beneficiaries under the 14th article of the treaty of 1830?

MARY CAROLINE ATKINSON

Not that I know of.

COMMISSION

Are you familiar with the law that empowers this Commission to hear and determine the rights of applicants for identification as Mississippi Choctaws?

MARY CAROLINE ATKINSON

I don't know much about it at all. I have never read much about it and don't know very much about it.

COMMISSION

Have you any documentary evidence showing that any of your ancestors ever complied with the provisions of that treaty either in receiving or claiming any lands in Mississippi as beneficiaries thereunder?

MARY CAROLINE ATKINSON

I don't think that my father ever received any land there or was ever benefitted by the lands there. If he was I—we always had a home there and lived there until we come out here. I know that he was very anxious for his family all to come to the Indian Territory.

COMMISSION

How did your father acquire possession of the land that he held in Mississippi?

MARY CAROLINE ATKINSON

He bought it.

COMMISSION

Did you or your father ever live in the Choctaw reservation in Mississippi?

MARY CAROLINE ATKINSON

I don't know. I don't think we ever did. Grand mother's people did.

COMMISSION

Do you make any claim by reason of any of the other provisions of the treaty of 1830?

MARY CAROLINE ATKINSON

I want a claim, if I am not accounted a Mississippi Choctaw I want to be recognized as a Choctaw because my grand mother was.

COMMISSION

What I am trying to find out from you is on what ground you are basing your claim. You have said that your names were not on any of the Choctaw rolls, that you were never admitted to citizenship in the Choctaw Nation. That you never made application to either the Choctaw tribal authorities or this Commission. You claim as a Mississippi Choctaw. You state that your father never received any land in Mississippi—

MARY CAROLINE ATKINSON

I said not that I knew of.

COMMISSION

And the Commission is unable to identify your father, whom you claim to have been on the 1830 roll in Mississippi as being there, and I want to know if you make your claim on any other treaty stipulations?

MARY CAROLINE ATKINSON

I don't know much about the treaties. I have never read anything about them and of course I don't know very much about it only what I have heard my ancestors say.

COMMISSION

Is there anything further you want to say in regard to your claim? Any material facts that you want to present to the Commission?

MARY CAROLINE ATKINSON

Nothing more only that my father was a part Choctaw. That is all the grounds I have. There may be a whole lot more but I don't know what it is. Of course I don't want to say anything only what I know to be so.

COMMISSION

Have you any children?

MARY CAROLINE ATKINSON

I have six.

COMMISSION

What are the names and ages of your children?

MARY CAROLINE ATKINSON

Don Ellis aged eight.

MARY CAROLINE ATKINSON

Cammie Elizabeth, seven.

MARY CAROLINE ATKINSON

Robert Pratt.

MARY CAROLINE ATKINSON

Sallie Bell.

MARY CAROLINE ATKINSON

Twins, Leonard and Lottie.

COMMISSION

Is there any written evidence you desire to offer the Commission in support of this application?

MARY CAROLINE ATKINSON

Yes sir.

RECORD

The original petition and application of Mary Caroline Atkinson, and affidavits of Elizabeth D. Bell, W. R. Collins, F. Atkinson, Margaret Elizabeth Williams, and T. E. Donaldson, are offered as Exhibit A, identified, filed, and made part of the record.

REGISTER

The oral examination ends. The Commission says its written decision will be mailed to Mary. Myra Young, the stenographer, certifies that she reported the October 23 proceedings in full and that the transcript is full, true and correct to her notes.

[silence]

A second family voice reaches some of the same objects. The field does not reset.

MUSKOGEE, INDIAN TERRITORY

October 23, 1900 · No. 879

MCR 879 Scene Two Kate De Bord · stenographer
REGISTER

William D. Bell is making this application for himself and four minor children. He is sworn. The transcript identifies the questioner only as “the Commission.”

COMMISSION

What is your name?

WILLIAM D. BELL

William D. Bell.

COMMISSION

What is your age?

WILLIAM D. BELL

51.

COMMISSION

What is your post office address?

WILLIAM D. BELL

Durant.

COMMISSION

You live at Durant?

WILLIAM D. BELL

Yes sir.

COMMISSION

How long have you lived at Durant?

WILLIAM D. BELL

Just came there a few days ago.

COMMISSION

Where did you live before you came to Durant?

WILLIAM D. BELL

In Texas.

COMMISSION

How long in Texas?

WILLIAM D. BELL

Since 1874.

COMMISSION

Where did you live before you lived in Texas?

WILLIAM D. BELL

Pontotoc County, Mississippi.

COMMISSION

How long did you live there?

WILLIAM D. BELL

Born there in 1849 and lived there until 1874.

COMMISSION

You claim to be a descendant of a Mississippi Choctaw do you?

WILLIAM D. BELL

Yes sir.

COMMISSION

Under which of your parents do you claim?

WILLIAM D. BELL

My father.

COMMISSION

What is your father's name?

WILLIAM D. BELL

Robert Bell.

COMMISSION

Living?

WILLIAM D. BELL

Dead.

COMMISSION

Did your father, Robert Bell, ever live in the Indian Territory?

WILLIAM D. BELL

He lived here two years, I believe, he came here two years before his death.

COMMISSION

What makes you think that you have a right now to be identified as a Mississippi Choctaw?

WILLIAM D. BELL

Finding my father's name on the Choctaw roll of 1830 makes me think so.

COMMISSION

Have you seen it there yourself?

WILLIAM D. BELL

No sir, I have never looked for it, but it is there though.

COMMISSION

What you know is what you have been told?

WILLIAM D. BELL

Yes sir.

COMMISSION

Do you know whether he ever took advantage of article 14 of the treaty of 1830?

WILLIAM D. BELL

No sir, if he did I never heard it.

COMMISSION

Do you know anything about the treaty of 1830 as it applies to your application?

WILLIAM D. BELL

I am not posted on that.

COMMISSION

Do you claim under the whole treaty or under article 14 of the treaty?

WILLIAM D. BELL

All I know is just finding my father's name. My understanding about it is that I could claim my right on his having been enrolled on that list of Mississippi Indian roll.

COMMISSION

Have you any documentary proof that he was a Mississippi Choctaw Indian and a beneficiary under the 14 article of the treaty of 1830?

WILLIAM D. BELL

Nothing only his being on that roll.

COMMISSION

What is your wife's name?

WILLIAM D. BELL

Martha Jane.

COMMISSION

Is she living or dead?

WILLIAM D. BELL

Dead.

COMMISSION

Where were you married to her?

WILLIAM D. BELL

In Mississippi.

COMMISSION

On what date.

WILLIAM D. BELL

Married in 1868.

COMMISSION

What day and month?

WILLIAM D. BELL

18th day of August.

COMMISSION

Have you your marriage license and certificate that you would like to offer as evidence?

WILLIAM D. BELL

I have not.

REGISTER

William's attorney asks for fifteen days to file additional documentary proof. The petition and affidavits are filed as Exhibit A. The Commission grants the request.

COMMISSION

Are there any others for whom you now make application besides yourself?

WILLIAM D. BELL

I have 4 children.

COMMISSION

What are their names and ages?

WILLIAM D. BELL

Clyde, 18; Mattie Jane, 15; William W. 13; Robert, he is 11.

COMMISSION

The basis of their claim is the same as yours?

WILLIAM D. BELL

Yes sir.

REGISTER

The oral examination ends. The Commission says its written decision will be mailed to William. Kate De Bord, the stenographer, certifies that she reported the proceedings in full and that the transcript is full, true and correct to her notes.

[silence]
