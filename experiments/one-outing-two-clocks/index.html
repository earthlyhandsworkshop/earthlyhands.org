Account one

Robert Bell

General Deposition No. 25 · sworn 9 May 1838

Bell describes Blackwell coming to his house and asking him to point out treaty-time residence places. In Bell’s account, the outing leaves from “Bell’s house.” He presents himself as a place-knower; that claim is source-local and is not film or Workshop validation of the places.

Account two

Blackwell

General Deposition No. 30 · sworn 8 May 1838

Blackwell says David Bell had been recommended as guide but could not be found, and Robert Bell was taken instead. He describes looking for section corners and half-mile marks, determining land numbers, writing them on the spot, and later reaching a limit of recollection without the memorandum made during the work.

The seam

Do not repair it.

These accounts are useful near one another because they expose different source roles inside related work. Bell supplies remembered place and guide knowledge. Blackwell supplies later field and survey translation, then marks where his own land-number recollection stops and documentary dependence begins.

The clock language does not line up neatly. The experiment leaves that pressure intact.

Related accounts ≠ one account.

Time difference ≠ error until a source earns that verb.

Older Robert body ≠ Bell–McKinney family Robert.

Years later, material from these older records was used inside the MCR 879 administrative record. That later use gives the 1838 objects another documentary life. It does not retroactively settle identity.
