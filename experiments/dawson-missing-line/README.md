# Dawson — missing-line visual experiment

**Status:** bounded visual experiment; does not replace the earlier Dawson inline-depth specimen  
**Public path:** `/experiments/dawson-missing-line/`  
**Ground:** Dawson / Southeast Reach → Blue Water  
**Passage:** True North / Mapping Grounds / EarthlyHands.org

## What changed

The earlier specimen proved that an earned relation can open locally before it carries a visitor away.

This continuation tests a different visual proposition:

> **The missing route can be allowed to occupy real visual space without being reconstructed.**

On desktop, the two source states are held apart by a large vertical interval.  
On a phone, that interval becomes a long body the visitor physically scrolls through.

The interval is intentionally **not scale** and **not geometry**. It is an interface refusal to invent the traveled line.

## What remains from the earlier experiment

- Dawson's approximate `about fifteen miles southeast` remains source language.
- The next source state remains an encampment on a small branch of Blue Water.
- `this creek` remains source-local backward continuity.
- Mayes and Criner remain source-local person occurrences.
- encounter, residence, and trapping remain distinct relations.
- opening a relation slip does not move the visitor.
- source descent remains progressive.
- the original manuscript has not yet been recovered.

## Visual grammar under test

### Absence may be visible

Blank or dark ground does not need to be filled with a speculative route.

### Screen distance is not historical distance

The long interval is experiential only. Its size makes uncertainty felt; it does not measure fifteen miles.

### Source states can be large

The interface lets Southeast Reach and Blue Water occupy the page as distinct states instead of compressing both into one card.

### Depth can stay marginal

Relation slips enter beside the prose. They do not replace the prose or turn every name into navigation.

## Device behavior

**Desktop:** source states use a fixed left rail and a large reading field; the missing-line interval is centered and spacious.

**Phone:** the left rail becomes a section header; the missing-line interval lengthens into a deliberate scroll; Ask becomes a small bottom control.

## Refusals

This specimen does not decide:

- the exact Dawson route;
- exact modern geometry for Blue Water or James' Fork;
- whether screen position should become a workshop-wide map language;
- whether other historical bodies should use this visual treatment;
- person identity beyond the source-local Mayes and Criner occurrences;
- whether this experiment should replace the earlier Dawson specimen.

The earlier specimen remains intact so the two can be compared rather than collapsed.
