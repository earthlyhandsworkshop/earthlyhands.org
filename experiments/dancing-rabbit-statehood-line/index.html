Dancing Rabbit → Oklahoma Statehood

This was the one.

What the drawing carries

Dancing Rabbit Creek; the Treaty of Dancing Rabbit Creek; removal west; Indian Territory; Oklahoma statehood, 1907.

It does not try to prove the exact route. It does not fill every blank. It does not pretend every point on the line is equally known.

That is part of why it still works.

What stayed with us

The strange, backward, almost-inside-the-map quality gave the passage a feeling we had not found before. It made the line feel less like a diagram laid over history and more like something you could enter and follow.

The picture is not perfect. We would probably draw parts of it differently now.

But this one hit.

It showed us that a drawing could be historically careful and still be beautiful; that blank ground could remain blank; that a thin line could carry a lot when every mark had a job.

Claim

A sparse line drawing can carry a long historical passage without pretending to settle every point along it.

Limit

Not a verified route map. Not an official house style. The drawing is kept because the specimen itself still deserves to stand.

A line is a claim.
A blank is not unfinished merely because it is blank.
