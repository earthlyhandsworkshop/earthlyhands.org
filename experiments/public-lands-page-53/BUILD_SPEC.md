# Public Lands as Inhabitable Ground

**Planning return · 26 September 2026**  
**Present body:** Public Lands No. 1, manuscript page 53  
**Standing proposition:** inhabitability without simulation

## 1. What is being built

Public Lands can become a documentary place whose geography is made from source-earned relations.

It is not a reconstruction of 1830s life. It does not invent streets, houses, routes, weather, dialogue, motives, or continuous historical scenes. It makes the surviving administrative object inhabitable by letting a visitor stand on one controlled page, notice what the page contains, follow only the relations the record actually carries, open depth without being moved, and return exactly.

The experience is not “Earthly Hands explains Public Lands.” It is “Earthly Hands gives the visitor enough trustworthy footing to begin reading Public Lands with us.”

The source remains the downward authority. The Earthly Hands controlled face is the ordinary working surface. The jacket carries why the Workshop reads it that way. Relations create possible doors. Uncertainty determines which actions are permitted.

## 3. Governing experience

The visitor meets a page with physical presence.

On desktop, it is a controlled working sheet laid slightly above a quiet table. The page has a finite body, edge, scale, and a small amount of depth. The manuscript is beneath it in the epistemic stack, not permanently beside it. The screen does not become a dashboard merely because space is available.

On a phone, the same object becomes a close-reading instrument. Rows and local depths are presented in a legible sequence rather than as a desktop table squeezed narrow.

The ordinary loop is:

1. **Stand** — arrive on one controlled page with a clear bearing.
2. **Notice** — names, marks, counts, localities, remarks, braces, correction states, and open edges can quietly declare that depth or reach exists.
3. **Inspect** — open a row, cell, mark, or derived operation without leaving the page.
4. **Follow** — take an earned relation to another occurrence, page, object, or instrument.
5. **Compare** — hold two source-local bodies beside one another without merging them.
6. **Ask** — ask from the exact present footing; deterministic answers resolve before the Worker is called.
7. **Return** — go back to the exact page, row, cell, scale, and opened depth from which movement began.

The visitor becomes capable through recurrence. After several pages, they begin recognizing the form's grammar without being told what every mark means. The deepest success is that the raw manuscript looks different when they return to it because the reader has changed.

## 4. Local physics

| Term | Meaning | What it permits |
|---|---|---|
| **Ground** | A bounded controlled body where the visitor can stand | Local reading, inspection, Ask, return |
| **Attention** | The visitor focuses on a row, cell, mark, relation, or instrument | Highlight and local depth; no relocation |
| **Depth** | More is shown about the present thing | Jacket, correction, provenance, source, arithmetic method |
| **Reach** | The source makes another occurrence, page, object, or question visible | A possible door; not proof of route or identity |
| **Movement** | The visitor deliberately crosses to another controlled ground | History entry, changed footing, exact return |
| **Landing** | The destination is controlled strongly enough to stand as ground | A new present body; not increased certainty by itself |
| **Comparison** | Two bodies are held together for a stated dimension | Difference without merge |
| **Source descent** | The historical representation opens beneath the controlled face | Inspection of the hand; no visitor relocation |
| **Open edge** | A relation, identity, reading, page, supplement, or route is named but unearned | A visible limit; no fabricated continuation |
| **Return** | Restoration of the visitor's prior footing | Same page, local anchor, depth, and useful scroll state |

Brakes:

- Reach is not movement.
- Movement is not historical travel.
- Landing is not certainty.
- Occurrence is not identity.
- Page adjacency is not household, kinship, ownership, or causation.
- A place-name occurrence is not precise geometry.
- Source sequence is not a continuous lived timeline.
- Development history is not epistemic history.
- A Worker response cannot create a door, promote a relation, or move the visitor.

## 6. Instruments

The page remains primary. Instruments are ways of seeing from the present ground.

- **Read** — controlled working face.
- **Account** — deterministic operations only.
- **Trace** — follows one controlled proposition or occurrence without identity inflation.
- **Map / Prose Map** — shows source-carried spatial relations only at earned precision.
- **Name Web** — occurrence-first; identity only when independently earned.
- **Jacket** — custody, correction, provenance, unresolved readings, nonclaims.
- **Source** — historical representation at the same host where possible.
- **Ask** — interprets only the supplied present ground; does not read the manuscript or create durable state.

## 8. Visitor state

Browser-owned state is separate from historical and Workshop claim state.

Keep only what improves orientation:

- present ground ID;
- local anchor;
- useful viewport / scale;
- earned local jacket reach;
- bounded back trail;
- Fresh Thread reset that clears conversational residue without erasing earned navigation depth.

Do not infer or persist intention, belief, learning, or historical conclusion from what a visitor opens or asks.

## 9. Deterministic browser / bounded Worker division

Browser and data own source/transcription state, anchors, inspections, arithmetic, relation types, doors, movement/return, correction state, provenance, and refusal to strengthen a relation.

The Worker may explain the controlled present body, answer from supplied deterministic facts, compose small connective prose among already reachable relations, and preserve an open edge.

The Worker may not inspect or adjudicate the manuscript image, calculate from uncontrolled cells, create routes, move the visitor, merge people or categories, convert reach into landing, or write durable shared state.

## 11. Failure tests

The build fails if the page becomes a dashboard, every mark is clickable, all information is available everywhere, a relation door implies historical movement, a same-name path implies identity, prose invents the connection it narrates, Ask performs source reading, source states collapse to generic empty, phone compresses the desktop table, opening depth loses the page, return loses the row, or page 54 outruns its controlled body.

## 12. Acceptance tests for the first build

1. identify Public Lands No. 1, manuscript page 53;
2. read the controlled page without opening the manuscript;
3. inspect Allen Yates without flattening layered statements;
4. inspect why 185 is permitted and why `1 + 12` is not added;

8. reach the manuscript at the same host;
9. return to the exact controlled footing;
10. encounter at least one honest open door that does not pretend the missing body is present.
