Carrier 01

Commission decision

15 Apr 1902

Direct administrative carrier · MCR 879 p.-55

Their Robert appears to comply.

The Commission first says one Elizabeth Bell affidavit does not show compliance or attempted compliance. It then separately says the applicants' historical material would make it appear that the Robert through whom they claim did comply.

Function here: Commission characterization of applicant-side historical proof.

Closest controlled state: Commission decision language on p.-55. “Appears” belongs to the Commission’s characterization; it is not a Workshop promotion of the historical proposition.

Carrier 02

Commission comparator

15 Apr 1902

Direct administrative carrier · MCR 879 pp.-55–56

A Robert Bell did comply.

The decision changes record body. It now speaks of a Robert Bell appearing in Commission-held records as compliant, then says that recorded Robert cannot be the applicants’ Robert.

Function here: positive comparator record used inside an identity exclusion.

Closest controlled state: Commission pp.-55–56. The record is positive for a Robert Bell. The adverse move is identity-conditioned, not a plain record-absence claim.

The open floor

What the line refuses to join.

Same words ≠ same referent.

“Robert Bell did comply” can recur while the administrative body changes which Robert it means.

Same fact ≠ same carrier.

A later certified patent can support the fact without becoming the object proved to have been before the earlier decisionmaker.

Upper review ≠ independent historical witness.

Commission, Indian Affairs, and Interior are separate administrative voices; repetition alone does not make three nineteenth-century witnesses.

Identity exclusion ≠ historical absence.

The adverse Robert move uses a positive comparator record and a government identity act. The Workshop does not convert that act into historical truth.
