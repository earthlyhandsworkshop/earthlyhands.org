# A cleared bench is not a published conclusion

Small Door · 27 October 2026 · provisional architecture bearing on draft PR #20

## Why this note exists

The existing fictional `bench-standing.mjs` records proposals, challenges, reviewed settlements, withdrawals, and the standing visible to different audiences. That is useful as a **standing-record specimen**, but it risks being mistaken for the lifecycle of the **Old-Growth Workbench itself**.

Those are different objects. A workbench is a temporary place of encounter, comparison, and earned understanding. It is not meant to become an endlessly active court, a permanent display shelf, or a central machine that controls all Workshop conclusions.

Prior Workshop clearing practice supplies the useful distinction: **end the summons without ending the thread**. Earlier Small Door clearing work proposed **clear by next verb, not by subject**. These are methodological bearings, not a new authorization to move any particular object or close any currently active inquiry.

## Three distinct crossings

1. **Inquiry closure — put the workbench away.** End a specific collection/examination window when its present work has become sufficiently intelligible or has reached a truthful bounded rest. Stop silently adding to or editing *that closed encounter*. Preserve what happened, including uncertainty. An unresolved matter need not be forced into consensus to leave the active table.
2. **Custody clearing — put each exact thing where it belongs.** A source, a locator, a copy, a paired representation, and a maker's own piece may require different operations and different permission. Leave one-to-one receipts and verify actual destinations before removing the active bench locator. A source may never need to move at all. Closing the inquiry itself authorizes neither a move nor disposal.
3. **Publication — authorize an outward statement or view.** This is separate. A settled or cleared bench does not publish anything, classify its products as canonical, transfer authorship, or authorize exposure of private sources. Public release, update, and withdrawal require their own bounded decision and source-permission review.

These may happen near one another, but **closing an encounter**, **moving its contents**, and **speaking publicly** are not interchangeable verbs. A single compulsory reviewer list or global truth committee is not established by the clearing practice.

## The closed record and the new arrival

Treat the closed encounter and its clearing receipts as dated history. Do not mutate them to pretend later knowledge was present earlier. If truly new source material, contradiction, or a new task earns attention, establish a *new* inquiry linked to the older record. It may reach a different warranted conclusion without erasing its predecessor. This is not a ban on future revision of knowledge; it is a boundary against silently rewriting the previous encounter.

A future interface should stop presenting a cleared bench as today's summons. Its findable return road may remain. No permanent extra doorway is required where an existing index and verified destinations already supply the address.

## Minimal fictional test before any implementation

- An encounter receives three invented arrivals: a locator to an unchanged original, a paired set under a known maker's custody, and a question that remains unresolved.
- Draw an explicit closing line for this encounter; a later arrival belongs to a new one.
- Release the locator only after verifying its original remains reachable; do not move that original.
- Move the paired set only under the appropriate permission, keeping both items and their relation visible.
- Rest the unresolved question under an exact address, **without** declaring it settled or discarding it.
- Verify each crossing; only then clear the active bench address and preserve the old index as a historical return path.
- Confirm that this operation changes **no** public release and that a later contrary arrival can begin a new linked inquiry without modifying the closed one.

Passing tests in the settlement ledger alone would **not** satisfy this workbench-lifecycle test.

## Open authority questions — intentionally not answered by code

- Who can declare this *particular collection window* complete? That may differ from who owns, moves, reviews, or publishes an individual contribution.
- Whose permission governs each actual custody crossing? How do we preserve a named unresolved rest when the answer is not yet available?
- Who authorizes an outward statement, with what evidence, scope, and record of review?
- How are private facts taken seriously during review without exposing their existence or substance to people without access?

Do not fill these questions with a default committee, a self-authorizing AI principal, a mandatory Ten approval on every item, or a fictionally authenticated `reviewedBy` field.

## Current engineering boundary

This note **does not change `bench-standing.mjs`** or assert that its existing `withdraw` event means physical clearing. The standing ledger remains a deliberately isolated in-memory test of projection and review receipts, **not** the authority, custody, or lifecycle engine for the real bench. Implementation should wait until the distinctions and actual governing permissions have earned a testable contract.

No production website change, migration, Google Drive move, publication, or real bench closure is authorized or performed by this note.
