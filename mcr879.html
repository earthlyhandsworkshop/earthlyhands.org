Two Bell proceedings.
Not one administrative road.

This specimen does not decide which Robert Bell was which. It places two federal Article XIV proceedings beside one another and keeps their administrative trajectories visible.

15 April 1902

MCR 879

The Bell–McKinney family proceeding

  1. Commission

    Identity refused.

    The Commission describes one affidavit as not showing compliance or attempted compliance, then separately acknowledges historical material that appears to support compliance, before turning to other records in its possession and making a Robert Bell identity exclusion.

    What this does not establish

    It does not establish that the family had no evidence of compliance, that historical nonperformance was proved, or that the Workshop adopts the Commission's Robert Bell identity conclusion.

  2. 19 April 1902

    The record moves upward.

    The Commission transmits the MCR 879 record to the Secretary of the Interior.

  3. Office of Indian Affairs · 13 May 1902

    A second federal record population enters.

    Indian Affairs re-examines the case using named federal carriers of its own and recommends affirmance.

    Why the distinction matters

    The Commission-held record population and the Indian Office record population should not be silently treated as one body merely because both are federal.

  4. Interior · 4 June 1902

    The refusal is affirmed.

    Acting Secretary Thomas Ryan states that the Department carefully reviewed the whole case and approves the Commission's action.

  5. Open authority edge

    Why was this §21 identity refusal reviewable here?

    Earlier administrative construction had treated §21 identity fact-finding as nonappealable. The source carrier explaining the change in review practice remains open.

27 September 1904

Wash / Jane / Sallie Bell

A later Bell proceeding under a changed regime

  1. Post-§41 environment

    The evidentiary architecture has changed.

    By 1904 the final Supplemental Agreement §41 structure exists. The decision separates applicants already treated within one evidentiary lane from other applicants carried through an older historical Article XIV route.

  2. Historical-person route

    The Commission assembles an affirmative chain.

    The decision carries ancestry, Ward signification, Murray–Vroom adjudication, land evidence, and Court of Claims material into an affirmative Robert Bell identity act.

    Identity brake

    The Workshop does not adopt that Robert Bell identity join. Robert and Robin remain source-bound forms unless a stronger bridge earns the relation.

  3. Commission

    Different applicants leave the decision in different states.

    Identification, settlement or removal proof, final-roll placement, and later enrollment are separate administrative states. One cannot stand in for another.

  4. Open review edge

    No matching Interior trajectory has yet been placed beside this identity act.

    The surviving workshop body presently does not carry a Secretarial review path comparable to the one visible in MCR 879.

Live outward question

What explains the difference between these administrative trajectories?

That question can be investigated without first deciding that either proceeding was correct, mistaken, fair, unfair, or about the same Robert Bell.

We are especially interested in surviving Nation, federal, family, and local records that can identify the record populations used, explain the review authority, or test the Bell family lines independently.

How to read this page

Source, comparison, and open question stay different.

This is a working specimen, not a finished historical conclusion. A source-carried act reports what a historical administrative body did or said. A workshop comparison places separate bodies beside one another. An open question remains open.

If stronger evidence changes one of these states, the page should change with it.
