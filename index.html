You are standing at the front edge of the workshop.

A lantern hangs nearby.

By lantern light

The light catches a report.

J. L. Dawson wrote from Cantonment Gibson on 29 January 1831. His report carries travel with the Choctaw and Chickasaw western exploring delegations.

The original manuscript has not yet been recovered. The Workshop presently reaches the report through its newspaper and later printed representations.

Dawson trail · during the night

A small guard is posted around the camp.

[

Two unnamed men have been sent to watch the ford of the river on the back trail.

The report does not say that they arrived.

]
  1. You, following the carried report.
  2. Dawson’s camp-side body, during the night.

Morning

Morning finds the party unattacked.

[

The camp remains where the report left it.

The two men, the ford, and the back trail remain out of view.

The source continues southeast.

]
  1. You, at the morning edge of the report.

The next travel leg

The party continues southeast about fifteen miles.

[

The report carries about fifteen miles of travel.

It does not preserve the line of the route.

A new camp lies ahead on a small branch of Blue Water.

]
  1. You, following the source-stated direction.
  2. Dawson’s party, traveling an approximate distance.

A new camp

A small branch of Blue Water.

[

On this creek, Dawson reports finding Mr. Mayes and Mr. Criner.

They reside on James’ Fork of Poteau.

They are trapping for beaver here. The report supplies no route between the creek and their residence.

]
  1. You, at the source-carried creek relation.
  2. Mayes and Criner, found on this creek in Dawson’s report.

The catch

They had caught but a dozen.

[

The dozen is one collective result for Mayes and Criner.

The report does not divide the catch between them or place it on a map.

Dawson says the weather was too cold to promise much further success.

]
  1. You, still following the report.
  2. Mayes and Criner, carried together in the stated result.

The trail continues. This first public passage stops here.
