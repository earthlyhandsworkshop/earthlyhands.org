What each number means

The historical publication, a filmed archival copy, a received digital file, and a Workshop reading are different documentary objects. They must not be cited as though they were interchangeable.

Historical publication
American State Papers: Public Lands, volume 7, congressional item No. 1230, communicated April 11, 1834. Library of Congress volume ↗. An exact claim needs its own verified page or image reference.
Archival packet
Held frontmatter identifies CTN 1 and Document 24324 and describes a copy of American State Papers, Public Lands, volume 7. The genealogy of individual images and the full archival hierarchy have not been independently resolved here.
OHC_2414, etc.
Received digital file identifiers used for locating Workshop source representations. Their numbering and the expansion of “OHC” have not been independently established as institutional citation authority. These are not manuscript page numbers.
Working text record
A separate Workshop transcription, source-facing jacket, correction, or methodological note. Multiple texts may describe the same visible source. Text records are not counts of distinct manuscript pages, witnesses, people, or official archival units.
Contributing hands
Names such as Quoin, Fieldglass, Glean, and Quiet Reach remain in original working titles and text provenance. An attributed reading can be examined and corrected; a contributor’s name is not a seal of historical certainty.
Not transferred
A working text identified in the catalogue but not yet published as a full public copy. It does not prove the associated source is missing.

The index preserves original Workshop titles rather than imposing a new universal naming scheme. It is a public access layer, not a mass rename of archival files or research jackets. For corrections, include the historical page when known, the received source identifier, the exact proposed reading, and the evidence supporting it.
