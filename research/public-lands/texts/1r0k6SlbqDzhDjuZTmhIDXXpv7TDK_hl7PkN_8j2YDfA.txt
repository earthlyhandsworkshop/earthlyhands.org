FIELDGLASS ADMINISTRATIVE CROSSING — WARD 20 JANUARY 1832 — THREE DISTRICT REGISTERS / ARMSTRONG RETURNS — 2026-09-09

ACTUAL SOURCE REOPENED
Library of Congress digital representation of American State Papers, Public Lands, vol. 7, No. 1230, `In relation to the location of reservations under the Choctaw treaty of the 27th of September, 1830`, printed p.127. This is a later federal printed compilation of the correspondence, not the original manuscript letter. Official return: https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/llscd/llsp034/llsp034.pdf


On 20 January 1832 William Ward, writing from Choctaw Agency to Secretary of War Lewis Cass, says he is enclosing `three registers of the three Choctaw districts` for reserve claims allowed by the three chiefs and Major Armstrong. He separately says copies of Armstrong's books had been left with him; that relinquishments were written opposite the relevant names under a `relinquished` column; that he retained a true copy of the original made out by Armstrong; and that the Department could compare the enclosed registers with `returns filed in the War Office by Major Armstrong`.


SOURCE-GENEALOGY CONSEQUENCE
The contemporary wording does not permit one undifferentiated `Armstrong record` object. At minimum the printed letter distinguishes: Armstrong books; copies of those books left with Ward; three district registers Ward sent to the Department; a true copy of an Armstrong-made original retained by Ward; and Armstrong returns already filed in the War Office. Exact physical identity among any of those bodies is NOT established by the letter.


For this jacket, the important crossing is administrative scope.
