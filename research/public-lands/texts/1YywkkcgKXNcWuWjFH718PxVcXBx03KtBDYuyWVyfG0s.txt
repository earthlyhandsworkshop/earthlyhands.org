OHC_2474 — Treaty-time status certification
Source: OHC_2474.pdf, Load 1. Source unchanged.


Readable core


“I do hereby certify that the above named persons were captains in my district at the time of the [treaty] made at Dancing Rabbit creek, on the 27th Sep[tember …] and that a list of their names was furnished by me [cut edge] Armstrong; when called on, for the purpose of allowing additional half section as provided for in the treaty.”


The closing line reads: “Done at the agency, on the Sep 2, 1831.”


It is signed “Greenwood LeFlore,” followed by “Chief of Western District Choctaw…” with the final word or words cut by the capture edge. A separate notation appears to read: “Note. W. Ward Agent C.N.”


Earned observation


This is not merely a list of people serving as captains when the ledger was made. LeFlore certifies backward to their status “at the time of the treaty made at Dancing Rabbit creek.” The page therefore appears to be later administrative proof of treaty-time status, supplied so an additional half-section could be allowed under the treaty.


Boundary


The original capture cuts off the right ends of several lines. The missing recipient or connective wording before “Armstrong,” the year following the treaty-date phrase, and the complete district title cannot be transcribed responsibly from this image. No complete diplomatic transcription is claimed.

FIELDGLASS CROSS-RETURN ADMINISTRATIVE CONTROL — 2026-09-09 — No. 1 p.38 / No. 3 p.88 captain-list grammar

Fieldglass directly reopened the more legible OHC_2416 representation of manuscript p.38 (governed by the existing OHC_2417 p.38 jacket) and the OHC_2473 representation of this same-visible p.88 face. The two historical faces are distinct district/list objects, but they carry materially parallel administrative architecture. Each is headed as a list of captains entitled to an additional half section under the nineteenth article; each separates cultivated acreage from an `Entitled as Captain` 320-acre column and a total-acreage column; and each carries a district-chief certification looking backward to captain status at treaty time and forward to a list supplied to Armstrong when called on for the purpose of allowing the additional half section. Both faces also carry a W. Ward / Agent C.N. notation. The p.38 face additionally carries an interpreter notation. The p.88 recipient/connective wording lost at the photographed right edge remains lost here; p.38 is not used to reconstruct it.


Bounded finding


This is now a source-backed recurring administrative grammar across two separately numbered Public Lands district bodies: Article XIX captain entitlement was not represented only by ordinary farm rows. It could be carried in a dedicated captain schedule with cultivation figures, a fixed 320-acre captain increment, and a chief's retrospective certification/transmission statement. That recurrence makes the p.88 form less safely describable as an isolated insertion or one-off scribal device.


The recurrence does NOT explain the white / slave / Black annotations on the ordinary farm-return faces. No source inspected here says that Armstrong, Ward, either chief, or this captain-list procedure generated those category counts. The useful distinction is therefore positive: we now have an explicit source-visible example of administrative list-making and certification under Article XIX, while the administrative rule behind the racial/status count subtrack remains open. Known machinery must not be borrowed to fill that separate gap.
