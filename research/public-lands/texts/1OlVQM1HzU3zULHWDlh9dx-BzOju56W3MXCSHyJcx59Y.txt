Material finding
Page 82 itself preserves two different documentary treatments of captain language:
1. `Capt Amos King.` carries the title directly in the name field.
2. PL:p82:r11 carries a name without a visible title while General Remarks separately states `Says he is Captain in place of ...`.


That difference is source-facing and useful for later name-column work. A title-bearing name form and a reported status statement must remain distinct data. This page does not authorize normalizing PL:p82:r11 to `Capt [name]`, nor does it authorize stripping `Capt` from Amos King’s written name form.


A second source-facing relation is also preserved: Amos King’s row explicitly says he `Lives with his father ...`. That is a source-stated father/co-residence relation on this row, not a corpus-wide identity bridge.


Interpretation earned for testing
The page demonstrates locally that office/status information can occur in different fields and with different evidentiary force. The name field can present a title as part of the written name form, while General Remarks can report a claim about status or succession. Later Name Web or column views should be able to preserve that distinction rather than collapsing both into one normalized title field.


This is a page-level structural observation only. It is not yet a rule for all Public Lands returns, captains, compilers, or periods.
