FIELDGLASS SOURCE-GENEALOGY DEEPENING — ROULLET PEARL RIVER MAP FAMILY — 2026-09-20


The earlier Fieldglass map-facing addendum correctly left the 1732 Roullet map as an external comparison target rather than using it to identify the Public Lands `Young Warrior` occurrence. A fresh return to the map’s archival-description genealogy materially sharpens that target without opening the map face itself.


Current Service historique de la Défense inventory control identifies Recueil 69, map 41 as Régis du Roullet’s 1732 `Carte du cours de la rivière aux Perles, depuis Boukfouka jusqu'à son embouchure, qui est à la passe à Dion vis-à-vis l'isle aux Oyes`, a 50 × 90 cm manuscript color map. The CCFr catalogue places the same object under SH 206 / old shelfmark 4040C and preserves an older box-number history. These are archival-description states for one map object, not independent geographic witnesses.


The same current SHD inventory identifies Recueil 69, map 42 as another 1732 Roullet manuscript color map: `Carte de la rivière aux perles, depuis son embouchure jusqu'à Boukfouka, et du chemin qui est depuis le fort de la Mobile jusques au dit Boukfouka`. Recent scholarship on the 1732 hydrographic mission explicitly associates R69-42 with R69-41 and describes it as probably a later but unfinished state. That description is a scholarly source-genealogy proposition, not a workshop inference from visual comparison; the faces still need to be opened and compared before any copying direction, revision relation, or toponym equivalence is promoted.


A nineteenth-century catalogue also describes a third Pearl River map in the same historical collection family: a later 1750 reduction of the Pearl River course, said to have been reduced from a map Philippe Buache made in December 1737 from Roullet's journal. Until that object is directly returned, carry it only as a catalogued later derivative candidate, not as another 1732 witness and not as a substitute for R69-41 or R69-42.


EARNED CHANGE: the next landscape-source door is no longer one isolated `Roullet map`. It is a controlled map-state family with at least two 1732 SHD objects that require face-to-face comparison, plus a catalogued later derivative branch. This matters because a like-named feature appearing on a later derivative cannot automatically be back-promoted to the same evidentiary state as a mark on R69-41.


NEXT EXACT VERB: obtain/view R69-41 and R69-42 at source-image depth; test each separately for a `Taskalamitta` / Young-Warrior-like inscription and its written relation to Pearl River, preserving spelling, placement, legend/cartouche information, and map-state differences. Only after that comparison should the Public Lands 1834 `Young Warrior` cluster be tested against either historical map occurrence. R69-43/later reduction remains a secondary genealogy branch unless directly recovered.
